Invoice finance for
recruitment and staffing

Hiring and staffing seems the classic invoice finance industry. You pay temporary workers, contractors, and even permanent placements weekly or biweekly; and clients settle on a monthly or bimonthly or even per order/project basis. Every new placement increases your payroll before revenue (i.e., you pay your employee before you get paid from your client). It feels like you are taking on high risks, especially in terms of cash flow, at the start of setting up your venture. Facilitation removes that risk; you fund the client invoice and get yourself covered up. Recruitment companies have a high working capital to sales ratio, usually two months' worth of working capital/sales outstanding. If we assume similar payment patterns across all sorts of businesses, staffing companies would typically have higher working capital requirements. Recruitment companies generally pay faster too, because payday is something you want your staff to be happy with, whereas your clients do not necessarily focus on paying on time, as long as it gets processed in due period of time. This seems to be a perfect fit for the recruitment industry. Many invoice finance solutions specifically cater to recruitment and payroll services and include additional features such as automated payroll services; some invoice solution providers automatically take care of the payroll of your employees. Recruitment and payroll companies tend to have large but numerous receivables that are repetitive in nature, hence they are easily manageable. Cash flow is not as easily planned for as with varying numbers of hours worked and different employees/placements on each occasion and varying wages.

Why cash flow is hard in recruitment

  • Payroll runs weekly or fortnightly regardless of client payment terms.
  • Timesheet disputes and approvals can delay client invoices by weeks.
  • Growth multiplies the problem: more placements mean more upfront payroll.
  • One slow paying client can consume the cash needed for all the others.

How a facility covers payroll

A solution with an invoice finance facility provides for advance payment of up to 90% of a client invoice. Advance payment occurs the minute the placement starts, timesheets are approved and payroll gets sent out on time, every time. The advance is settled on the client paying and funds become available on every new placement, so taking on a bigger client is an opportunity because there is funding readily available.

A worked example

The client agency invoices £50,000 to the client for temporary staff during this month on 30-day terms. Their payroll is also due this week.

The covers are paid for, margin secured, and the agency can continue placing

Who qualifies

Business owners with creditworthy and approved time sheets for candidates they place can use the facility; from single agencies to multi-site staff firms, if you invoice businesses on terms, this can work!

Common questions

Can I fund invoices before timesheets are approved?

A provider generally needs the approved time sheet and a signed agreement to access funding. Disputing time on a time sheet or unapproved time on a time sheet is harder to fund.

How does it work with weekly payroll?

The client invoice advance lands in your bank 24 hours later, so you can pay your contractors weekly or bi-weekly while the client pays you at 30 days plus.

What if a placement ends early?

Advise provider right away: Credits and adjustments are done during reconciliation, where the facility will be recalculated on the remaining ledger.
