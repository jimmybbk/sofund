Invoice finance for
manufacturers

Manufacturers will fund raw materials, production runs and staff long before they ever see a cent from the customer. The bigger an order, the bigger the gap, and often the biggest working capital will be the order most worthwhile. Therefore, an invoice finance facility will help cash-fund this to help the manufacturers continue the next run, never having to wait for the money received from previous runs.

Why cash flow is hard in manufacturing

  • Raw materials are bought on short supplier terms, often 14 to 30 days.
  • Customers pay at 60 to 90 days, and export customers can be slower still.
  • Large orders consume working capital that the next run needs.
  • Staff, energy and equipment costs do not pause between orders.

How a facility funds production

A facility advances up to 90% of the value of your finished invoice within 24 hours. You buy materials for the next production run and cover payroll while the customer pays on their terms. Take orders worth more without your wondering whether you have the necessary working capital.

A worked example

Your factory finished one order and invoiced customer £150,000 in 90 day terms. Next run's materials and labour need to be now ordered, which means some resources are now tied down for 90 days. Next production run will result in sales proceeds of £80,000. In average, materials, labour, overheads and profit consist of this sales amount as the following proportions - Materials 30%, Labour 40%, Overheads 20% and Profit 10%. Overheads paid by cash is proportion of total £80,000. 30% materials are on 90 days terms, labour is on 45 days. Assume that for both next run orders, the overheads, labour, materials and other expenses are in proportion to their sales values. Assuming this, we work out as followings; Materials 30%: £150,000*30% = £45,000 on 90 days terms Labour 40%: £150,000*40% = £60,000 cash 45 days. Overheads payable in cash 20%: £150,000*20% = £30,000 Profit 10%: Profits on sales will not be able to realised in cash, because of 90 day terms, therefore not involved in cash payments calculations. Profit was (£150,000*10% = £15,000 which will now not be realised now in cash). Next run sales proceeds £80,000, which has not yet been received. Materials: (£80,000*30% = £24,000 on 90 days terms) Labour £80,000*40% = £32,000 on 45 days terms) Overheads £80,000*20% = £16,000 in cash. Summary of cash outflow during this week for production orders are; Materials - £45,000+£24,000 = £69,000 on 90 days terms Labour - £60,000+£32,000 = £92,000 on 45 days terms Overheads - £30,000+£16,000 = £46,000 payable instantly with cash Total amount of cash needed during this week; Labour expenses for future £92,000, Overheads for future £46,000, Total cash needed; £92,000+£46,000 = £138,000 This situation will create an adverse affect in the company's internal working capital; since all its assets including accounts receivable, inventory and equipment may now decrease, without the appropriate increase in revenues. Its liabilities too can increase.

Who qualifies

Invoices due from business customers for goods delivered, undisputed debt, a creditworthy customer, a facility can be and is used by component makers through to full-scale manufacturers.

Common questions

Can I fund an invoice before delivery?

Some providers fund against goods delivered in arrears; if it is a long production, it might be possible to make an arrangement for progress or deposit payments - do check before signing.

What do providers check?

Received and Accepted; of Good credit; Unchallenged.

Does it work for export customers?

Mostly yes, with some difference of advance rate for domestic customers vs. overseas. Do confirm with your provider before quoting anything.
