How much does invoice factoring cost?

Zap Funds | 9 August 2026 | 6 min read

The truthful response is "it depends". Factoring fees are not just one single rate; instead, they are based upon your sales, your clients, and the time taken for an outstanding invoice to be paid. With invoice factoring, we can give you details of each individual charge that adds up to your total amount, the things that encourage higher charges, and how you should be distinguishing various providers to locate the right one for your organisation.

The three fees that make up the cost

1. The discount or funding fee

Providers commonly express it as a rate per 30 day period, with additional charges if your customer takes longer. Better customer credit quality and shorter payment terms mean lower rates.

2. The administration fee

This covers the running of the facility: processing invoices, managing the ledger, and handling collections if the arrangement includes them. It can be a flat monthly amount or a percentage of the funds drawn.

3. The establishment fee

Setting up fee: This is a once off payment at the set up stage and covers the onboarding, the checks, and documentation.

What drives your rate up or down

  • Customer quality: invoices to large, creditworthy businesses with a record of paying are cheaper to fund than invoices to unknown customers.
  • Time outstanding: invoices paid in 30 days cost less to fund than invoices that run to 90.
  • Volume: higher and consistent invoicing earns better rates.
  • Industry: some industries carry higher risk and price accordingly.
  • Disputed invoices: contested claims are harder to fund and can attract higher fees.

A worked example

Assume they factor $80,000 of invoices each month at a funding cost of 2% per 30 day period plus charges a $500 monthly administration fee:

ItemAmount
Invoices factored$80,000
Advance releasedUp to 90% of face value
Funding fee at 2%$1,600
Administration fee$500
Total monthly cost$2,100

This is illustrative only. All providers price differently; therefore, compare the quotes that you receive.

How to compare providers properly

Providers structure fees differently, so compare the total cost for the same scenario rather than any single number. Ask each provider for:

  • The funding fee rate, and whether it steps up the longer an invoice runs
  • All administration and establishment charges
  • What happens to fees if your customer pays late or disputes the invoice
  • Minimum usage or contract terms, and the notice to exit

Then compare the total monthly cost at your actual invoicing volumes, not the headline rate.

Is factoring worth the cost?

The honest comparison is against the alternatives: lost early payment discounts from your own suppliers, interest on overdrafts, credit card rates, declined contracts, or payroll stress. For businesses with strong customers and long payment terms, the fee usually costs less than the opportunities missed while waiting. For businesses with thin margins and unreliable customers, it may not stack up, and an honest broker will say so.

Frequently asked questions about factoring costs

How much does invoice factoring cost?

Costs are usually quoted as a discount fee on the amount funded, commonly expressed as a rate per 30 day period, plus administration and establishment fees. The total depends on your volumes, customers and how long invoices stay outstanding.

Why does factoring cost more than a bank loan?

The provider carries the credit risk of your invoices and manages collections, and advances in days rather than weeks. You are paying for speed and flexibility.

Do fees increase if my customer pays late?

Usually yes. Funding fees accrue for the time the invoice stays outstanding, so a 90 day invoice costs more to fund than a 30 day one.

How do I get the lowest rate?

Compare written offers from multiple providers, keep your invoices clean and undisputed, and work with creditworthy customers. A broker comparison shows every rate side by side.
