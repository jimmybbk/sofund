Invoice finance is having a moment.

10 November 2026 · 5 min read · Zap Funds

Every few years, a funding product stops being a niche and becomes a movement. In 2026, invoice finance is that product — and the data behind it is worth every business owner's attention.

The numbers behind the surge

Start with the big picture. The OECD's latest Financing SMEs and Entrepreneurs scorecard shows new lending to Australian small and medium businesses rose from $122.5 billion in 2023 to $153.7 billion in 2024 — a jump of roughly 25% in a single year. Businesses aren't just borrowing more; they're borrowing differently.

Globally, the invoice financing market was valued around US$3.9 trillion in 2026, with analysts at Mordor Intelligence projecting growth to nearly US$4.8 trillion by 2031. Australia is a meaningful part of that curve, and the direction is unmistakable: more businesses are discovering that the fastest money they'll ever access is already sitting in their own accounts receivable.

Why now? Three forces converging

1. Late payments are still the default

QuickBooks' 2026 late payments research found 59% of businesses carry invoices more than 30 days overdue — up from 47% the year before — with an average of $17,700 sitting in limbo. When your customers stretch to 60 and 90 days, waiting politely stops being a strategy.

2. Selective invoice finance went mainstream

The old model — factor your whole ledger, every invoice, forever — is giving way to selective facilities where you fund a single invoice, or a handful, only when you need the cash. You keep control of customer relationships and dip in only when timing demands it.

3. Technology collapsed the timelines

Lenders are using AI-driven underwriting to cut approval times by as much as 80%. Facilities that once took weeks of paperwork now routinely turn around in a day or two. Speed used to be the trade-off for flexibility; in 2026 it's the whole point.

What this means for your business

If you invoice other businesses on 30, 60 or 90-day terms, the surge in invoice finance demand is not a fad — it's the market pricing in a simple truth: cash today beats cash in ninety days. The businesses winning with it in 2026 tend to share three habits:

  • They fund growth invoices, not distress — using the facility to take bigger jobs, not just plug gaps.
  • They compare written offers from multiple lenders instead of accepting the first quote.
  • They keep their banking relationships intact, because invoice finance works alongside the bank, not instead of it.
