What Is Invoice Finance? How It Works and Who Uses It

Zap Funds | 21 November 2026

You have done the job, you have sent the invoice, now you are waiting. Maybe a month or two, sometimes three. They are happy with your invoice, it is legally binding, but that cash is languishing in their till and you are running on empty.

That would be that problem that invoice finance aims to alleviate.

In terms of statistics and figures, the scale of this problem is significant. In a typical Australian small business, an outstanding invoice takes an average of 24 days to be paid (Xero Small Business Index), or as many as 6-8 days after the invoice becomes overdue. Late payments cost Australian small businesses around $1.1 billion per year, with the problem worsening over the last few years, with CreditorWatch having found that approximately one in ten invoices remain outstanding by the 60-day mark. The insolvency of Australian businesses is also most frequently down to cash flow problems, and that is also an issue which has regularly made its way to the forefront of ASIC's insolvency statistics for several years."

So what is it, exactly?

Invoice finance is an alternative to waiting for your invoices to be paid within the payment term, where you can take the appropriate invoice to a finance provider, they then pay you up to 70/90% of the invoice value. Once the invoice is settled by the invoice payer the finance provider retains the fee and remits the balance.

You are not taking out a loan to buy equipment or a home as in a bank, you are using your credit that is coming in and the funds are tied up with the receivable. This is why it is called debtor finance or, and perhaps more accurately, receivables finance.

These are two main variations, and the distinction is an important one.

Invoice factoring - the factor takes control of the invoices and also often the administration of getting payment. The supplier is advised ("disclosed") and pays the factor directly. As such all the sales ledger control, credit checking and chasing is performed for the business which is why this is a popular service in small business (who usually have no credit control staff).

Invoice discounting. The discounting is all carried out in the background; your business goes about collecting receivables and issuing invoices as before. It is usually the case that your account holders will be completely oblivious to the fact and in fact the discounting company usually requires secrecy so this is kept strictly confidential. As this is the case, it tends to be more appropriate for larger businesses with in-house credit control.

Then there is the issue of risk. Most of the places in Australia deal "with recourse" that is to say if your customer does not pay it goes against you. With "non recourse" the credit risk is passed onto the other party but they charge you more and have certain rules. Worth finding this out before signing on the dotted line as it is the difference between you getting your money or not when it really matters.

How it works, step by step

Your business is providing a product or service to another business.

An invoice is sent out according to the agreed payment terms.

The finance providers will examine the invoice and customer and will, if satisfied, pay you a percentage of the value of the invoice.

The customer pays the invoice.

Your balance will be processed by the bank account's owner. The bank account's owner will take a commission and then pay the rest to you.

There are different methods; one-off facilities - you submit one invoice, you are funded once and another on a rolling basis against your sales ledger - every invoice you submit increases the amount available (in effect, working capital as you need it and at varying speed of implementation, from 24 hours (when crisis hits) to the next day (when you are happy again).

What does it cost?

The rate varies. As the majority of agencies charge a discount on the money they make the invoice at, factoring and the administration of it all. The price depends on the amount you are factoring, how long the debtor will take to pay you, their credit quality and if there is recourse on the invoice.

What is fair is this. Compare costs. When you add it all up and you are choosing between waiting 60 days until you receive the cash or not going into the next contract/missing some super/putting material on a 20% credit card and factoring starts to look like a good idea.

Who uses invoice finance?

This applies to any entity that sells to or invoices another entity, in anticipation of collection of those invoices; and usually applies to the "guilty parties" as follows

Construction and trades - These folks have got it all figured out. With a main contractor and a host of subcontractors waiting on the end of a phone, the main payments (wages, super, materials etc) will be flowing out once a week and therefore these guys can be found waiting in queues at the court. In 2024-25 construction accounted for over 26% of all Australian firm insolvencies and the common story here also includes delayed payments. We have talked in depth about their issue of late payments in our construction invoice factoring guide.

Traffic and freight. The fuel and bridge tolls just will not stop and if your customer is taking 45 days to pay, those wages are not going to wait on your invoice. The traffic sector lends itself well to the high level and predictability of invoices generated.

Manufacturing - pay for your materials first, get your goods ready, ship them off when the customer requests and then receive payment on the agreed terms. This gap is where production goes on to meet the orders received.

Recruitment and staffing. A classic situation in the factoring industry; you pay contractors and temporary employees weekly or fortnightly, yet your customers settle the invoices monthly or quarterly. As such you need to finance your own payroll and can rely upon invoice finance to do so.

Wholesale and distribution

We sell product, we issue invoice to the retailers, the retailers pay us when they feel like it and we use those receivables to restock.

IT and professional services. Contractors and consultancies; short to medium term engagements in the business (typically 30-60 days) and pay them long before getting any cash back.

Will my business qualify?

Each supplier is different, but the pattern is the same. You need a good credit profile for the customer, invoices (not customer statements) from other businesses, the debt does not need to be in dispute and you must prove that the work was undertaken. It is available across virtually any sector, whether small scale or multinational. Since the funding is taken directly from your invoice and not your asset the property is generally unimportant.

And these are the sorts of things - sending invoices to customers for goods and services they dispute or doing all business with one client who is struggling financially or trading whilst insolvent, that one should pick up on fairly easily.

Common questions

How much can I access? The percentage of your invoices which you can access is usually between 70% to 90% and your facilities, in line with the growth of your sales ledger, can extend into the millions.

How fast is it? The first part of the process (getting your approval) will not be too swift and you will need to supply a range of information (account details, customer details and examples of invoices) however, once the system is live and you have your approval, you can request an advance on new invoices (on the day or the following day!).

Will my customers find out? Yes, they will if you do factoring, otherwise they pay the organisation directly. This means if you are in a position where your customer relationships are confidential, discounting could be the better option.

What happens if a customer does not make their repayments? There are several factors, but in short if it is a recourse facility the money could come back to you; and if it is a non-recourse facility there are some credit risks (with certain conditions and exclusions) included in the facility.

Can it be used in addition to a bank loan or overdraft? This is often the case; however, it is possible that your bank will have a charge on the receivables, so it is advisable to clarify this issue with both of the two parties involved.

Purpose - suitable for a small businessInvoice finance is ideally suited to the small business that does a lot of work with other businesses, but gets late payments, which then prevents growth because of cash flow - sounds familiar? - then invoice finance is perfectly tailored to your business.
