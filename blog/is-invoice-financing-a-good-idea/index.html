Is invoice financing a good idea?

Zap Funds | 28 July 2026 | 6 min read

Invoice financing depends on the business. Those that tell you otherwise are selling something. Invoice financing is a great funding option for one company and, at the same time, an unfit solution for others. Here, we explore in which case scenario invoice financing is a good funding option, when it does not fit, and which are the considerations that you need to take when choosing the funding.

When invoice financing is a good idea

You invoice other businesses on payment terms. Invoice financing funds business to business invoices. If your customers are companies paying at 30, 60 or 90 days, the money you are waiting for is exactly what a facility unlocks.

Growth is straining your cash. Winning bigger contracts means bigger upfront costs: materials, payroll, hire equipment. Funding against the invoices those contracts generate turns growth into an opportunity instead of a cash flow risk.

Customers pay late and it hurts. Waiting on 60 day invoices while wages run weekly is the classic reason businesses look for finance. The advance settles when the customer pays, so there are no fixed loan repayments fighting a quiet month.

You do not want property security over the business. Most invoice finance facilities use the invoices as security. If keeping your property out of the arrangement matters to you, this is a cleaner structure.

You want to keep your bank lines free. Facilities commonly run alongside existing bank accounts and loans, leaving your bank credit available for other purposes.

When invoice financing is not a good idea

You sell directly to consumers. Invoice finance funds business to business invoices. If your customers are the general public paying on delivery, this product is not built for you.

  • Your invoices are regularly disputed. Providers assess invoice validity, and disputed claims are harder to fund until resolved.
  • Your margins cannot carry the fees. Factoring costs money, and thin margin work may not leave enough room. Compare the total fees against the margin on the funded work.
  • You need money for a long term purchase. Equipment, vehicles and property are better funded with loans matched to the asset's life.
  • Your real problem is unprofitable pricing. Funding fixes timing, not margins. If every job loses money, financing more of them makes the losses arrive faster.

    • The honest advantages

    • Cash within 24 hours of issuing eligible invoices
    • Up to 90% of the invoice value advanced
    • No property security in most facilities
    • Funding that grows with your sales ledger
    • Confidential options where customers keep paying you directly

    The honest disadvantages

    • Fees are higher than bank loan interest rates, because the provider carries the collections and the credit risk
    • Your customers deal with the provider under factoring arrangements
    • Recourse facilities can leave your business responsible if a customer fails to pay
    • The facility only funds invoices that are valid and undisputed

    The questions that decide it

    Ask yourself these four questions honestly:

    1. Is the work profitable? Funding fixes timing, not margins.
    2. Are my customers creditworthy businesses? The invoice is the security.
    3. Would earlier cash let me take more work, or pay less late fees and interest elsewhere?
    4. Do the fees cost less than the opportunities or discounts I am missing while I wait?

    If the answers point yes, then they will recommend that you obtain written options in comparison from several providers. Look at them side by side. The answers are within the numbers.
