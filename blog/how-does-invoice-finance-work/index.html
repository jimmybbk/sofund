How does invoice finance work in Australia?

Zap Funds | 6 min read | 21 November 2026

You finish the job. You send the invoice. And then your money sits in your customer's bank account for 30, 60 or 90 days while payroll, suppliers and the tax office all keep their original deadlines.

Invoice finance fixes that timing problem. Instead of waiting for the customer to pay, a finance provider advances you most of the invoice value up front, then collects the balance when your customer settles up. This guide walks through exactly how it works in Australia, what it costs and how to know whether it suits your business.

What is invoice finance?

Invoice finance is business funding secured against your unpaid invoices, which is why it is also called debtor finance or receivables finance. A finance provider advances you a percentage of an invoice's value, usually up to 90%, and releases the remaining balance, minus fees, once your customer pays.

Because the invoice itself acts as the security, property guarantees are generally not required. And because the funding follows your sales, the amount you can access grows whenever your invoicing grows.

How invoice finance works, step by step

Step 1: Complete the work

You supply goods or services to a business customer and agree on payment terms, commonly 30, 60 or 90 days.

Step 2: Issue the invoice

You send a valid invoice for the completed work. In construction this is usually a progress claim prepared under your state's Security of Payment laws, and a properly served claim matters later when the provider assesses it.

Step 3: Submit the invoice for funding

You send the invoice to your finance provider, typically through an online portal, along with the supporting documents: contracts, purchase orders, delivery confirmations or certificates.

Step 4: The provider assesses it

The provider checks that the work was delivered, that the invoice is genuine and that your customer is creditworthy. This is the step that decides the advance rate and the fees.

Step 5: You receive the advance

Once approved, the provider pays you up to 90% of the invoice value, often within 24 hours of submission. The money is yours to use: wages, materials, suppliers, tax, the next project.

Step 6: Your customer pays the invoice

Depending on the facility, your customer pays you or pays the provider directly.

Step 7: You receive the balance

The provider subtracts its fees from the invoice amount and releases the remainder to you. The transaction is complete, and the same cycle can repeat with every new invoice.

What it looks like in dollars

Imagine a recruitment agency that has placed five temp staff with a client. It issues one invoice for $60,000 on 30-day terms, but its own workers are paid weekly. With an 80% advance rate:

ItemAmount
Invoice value$60,000
Advance at 80%$48,000 paid within 24 hours
Balance held until the customer pays$12,000
Customer pays the invoice on day 30$60,000
Balance released to you, minus feesThe remaining $12,000

That is $48,000 this week instead of next month. The advance rate and fees vary between providers, which is exactly why comparing written offers matters.

The two main types of invoice finance

Invoice factoring

The provider takes over the collections: your customers are notified and pay the provider directly. Suits businesses without an accounts team that want chasing handled for them.

Invoice discounting

Confidential. Your customers keep paying you as normal and usually never know a finance company is involved. Suits established businesses with their own credit control.

Selective invoice finance

Fund a single invoice, or a handful, instead of the whole ledger. Useful when one large contract creates a one-off cash flow gap. Not every provider offers it, so check before committing.

What does invoice finance cost?

Providers generally charge three kinds of fees:

  • Discount or funding fee: the main cost, based on the amount funded and how long the invoice stays outstanding.
  • Administration fee: for running the facility and processing invoices.
  • Establishment fee: a one-off charge when the facility is set up.

The total depends on your turnover, the quality of your customers and how long invoices take to pay. Always ask for the full cost breakdown in writing, and ask what happens to the fees if your customer pays late.

Invoice finance or a business loan?

They solve different problems. A loan provides a fixed sum with fixed repayments, which suits long-lived purchases like equipment. Invoice finance moves money you have already earned into your account sooner, and the funding grows as your invoicing grows.

Neither is automatically better. The question is whether your problem is the total amount of money, or the timing of it. Many businesses use both at once.

Who qualifies for invoice finance?

You need to invoice other businesses on payment terms, the invoice must be for work genuinely completed, the debt must not be in dispute, and your customer needs to be creditworthy. That covers businesses of every size across construction and trades, transport and logistics, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, recruitment and staffing, cleaning, engineering and professional services.

The industries we see most often are the ones with the longest payment chains. Construction alone accounted for over a quarter of Australian company insolvencies in 2024-25, and subcontractors bear most of that weight. Our construction invoice factoring guide covers that industry in detail.

Frequently asked questions

How quickly can I get money against an invoice?

The first approval takes the longest, because the provider assesses your business and your customers. After that, advances against new eligible invoices are typically funded within 24 hours.

How much of an invoice can I access?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of an eligible invoice's value, and your available funding rises as your sales ledger grows, sometimes into the millions.

Do I need property security?

Generally no. The invoice is the security. Some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets, so confirm exactly what is required before signing.

Will my customers know?

With invoice discounting, usually not: your customers keep paying you directly and the arrangement stays confidential. With invoice factoring, customers are notified because the provider manages the collections.

Can small businesses qualify?

Yes. Size matters less than the invoice: if it is genuine, issued to a business customer and not in dispute, small operators can qualify alongside large ones.

What happens if my customer does not pay?

That depends on recourse. With a recourse facility the risk sits with your business. Non-recourse facilities carry more of the customer credit risk themselves but cost more and come with conditions.
