How Does Invoice Finance Work in Australia? A Step-by-Step Guide for Businesses

By Wayne Clothier | Zapfunds | 3-minute read

Invoice finance is a way for Australian businesses to obtain money that is tied up in customer invoices before the customers make payment. Of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days, businesses can receive an advance on qualifying invoices. Invoice finance helps with cash flow. Can be used to cover everyday running costs.

For businesses that feel the squeeze on cash flow, invoice finance can provide working capital without relying on business loans or property‑backed security.

What Is Invoice Finance?

Invoice finance is a type of business funding that allows companies to borrow against or receive an advance on customer invoices that remain outstanding.

When a business supplies goods or services to another business, it normally sends an invoice with payment terms of 30, 60 or 90 days.

The sale is completed,. The business must wait for payment before it can access that money.

Invoice finance fills this gap by allowing businesses to access a portion of the invoice value earlier.

Simply put, invoice finance transforms invoices into working capital.

How Does Invoice Finance Work? (Step by Step)

The invoice finance process is fairly simple once the funding facility has been approved.

Here is how it works with Zapfunds.

Step 1 – Submit Your Invoices

After you supply goods or services to your customers, you send your invoices.

When you need working capital, you submit outstanding invoices through the Zapfunds online portal.

You can choose the invoices you want to finance, subject to the facility agreement and eligibility requirements.

Step 2 – Receive an Advance

Once your invoices have been reviewed and approved, Zapfunds can advance up to 85 percent of the invoice value, subject to approval and facility terms.

Depending on the facility and verification requirements, funding may be available within 24 hours.

This gives your business access to working capital without waiting for customers to pay.

You can use the funds to:

  • Pay employee wages and subcontractors
  • Purchase materials and stock
  • Cover operating expenses
  • Fund contracts and projects
  • Manage supplier payments
  • Support business expansion

Step 3 – Your Customer Pays the Invoice

Your customer continues with their payment process.

Depending on whether the facility operates as invoice factoring or invoice discounting, payments may be collected by the finance provider. Managed by your business.

The customer payment is then applied to the invoice finance balance.

Step 4 – Receive the Remaining Balance

Once your customer pays the invoice, the transaction is reconciled.

The remaining invoice balance is released to your business after deducting the advance and applicable fees.

This completes the invoice finance transaction.

What Are the Different Types of Invoice Finance?

There are two types of invoice finance to Australian businesses: invoice factoring and invoice discounting.

What Is Invoice Factoring?

Invoice factoring is a funding arrangement in which a business sells or assigns its invoices to a finance provider.

The finance provider generally manages the collection process. Receives payment directly from customers.

Invoice factoring can suit businesses that:

  • Want to outsource accounts management
  • Have limited internal administration resources
  • Want to reduce the time spent chasing invoices
  • Need working capital
  • Customers are generally aware of the arrangement because payments are directed to the finance provider

What Is Invoice Discounting?

Invoice discounting allows businesses to access funding against invoices while generally retaining responsibility for customer collections.

The business continues managing its accounts and customer relationships.

Invoice discounting can suit established businesses that:

  • Have an existing accounts team
  • Prefer to manage customer relationships
  • Want confidentiality around their funding arrangements
  • Have business‑to‑business invoicing

The availability of confidential invoice discounting depends on the provider and facility agreement.

What Is the Difference Between Invoice Finance and a Business Loan?

Invoice finance and traditional business loans both provide access to working capital. They work in ways.

Invoice FinanceTraditional Business Loan
Funding is linked to unpaid invoicesFunding is generally provided as a lump sum or agreed credit limit
Available funding can increase with eligible salesFunding is determined by the loan agreement
Repayment is generally linked to customer invoice paymentsRepayments usually follow an agreed schedule
Assessment considers invoices, customers and business circumstancesAssessment considers creditworthiness and other lending criteria
Property security may not be requiredSecurity requirements vary by lender and loan

Invoice finance can be especially useful for businesses that are growing, because more sales can open up funding.

However, the best option depends on your business circumstances, your funding requirements and the cost of each facility.

How Much Does Invoice Finance Cost in Australia?

Invoice finance is not money. Providers usually charge fees for giving access to working capital and for running the facility.

Common costs can include:

1.. Discount fee – A fee tied to the funding provided. It may depend on the amount, the funding period and the facility structure.

2. Administration fee – A fee that covers the administration and management of the facility.

3. Establishment fee – A fee that may apply when the funding facility is set up.

The actual cost depends on things such as your business turnover, invoice quality, customer payment history, industry and facility requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions About Invoice Finance

1. How quickly can I receive invoice finance?

Depending on the provider, facility approval and invoice verification requirements, funding may be available within 24 hours. Existing approved facilities can provide access to working capital.

2. How much of an invoice can I borrow against?

Some invoice finance providers offer advances of up to 85 percent of an invoice value. The actual advance rate depends on the provider, invoice eligibility and facility terms.

3. Do I need property security for invoice finance?

Many invoice finance facilities do not require property as security. However, providers may require security over business assets, receivables or other guarantees depending on the facility.

4. Does my customer know I am using invoice finance?

It depends on the type of facility. Invoice factoring generally involves customer notification, while confidential invoice discounting may allow your business to manage collections without disclosing the arrangement.

5. Can small businesses use invoice finance?

Yes. Medium‑sized businesses may be eligible if they issue qualifying business‑to‑business invoices and meet the provider’s assessment requirements.

6. Can I finance invoices?

Depending on your facility, you may be able to finance invoices or a broader portion of your sales ledger.

7. What happens if my customer doesn't pay?

The consequences depend on whether the facility's recourse or non‑recourse and on the terms of your agreement. Under a recourse arrangement, your business may remain responsible for invoices.

8. Is invoice finance better than a business overdraft?

Neither option is automatically better. Invoice finance is linked to invoices. May grow with eligible sales, while an overdraft provides access to an agreed revolving credit limit. The right option depends on your cash flow requirements and funding costs.

9. Can invoice finance help my business grow?

Yes. By releasing cash tied up in invoices, businesses may have working capital available to take on new contracts, purchase stock, pay suppliers and manage increasing operating expenses.
