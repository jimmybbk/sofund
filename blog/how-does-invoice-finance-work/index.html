How Does Invoice Finance Work in Australia? A Step-by-Step Guide for Businesses

By Wayne Clothier | Zapfunds | 3-minute read

Invoice finance is a way for Australian businesses to get money that is stuck in customer invoices before those customers pay. Of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days to get paid, businesses can get an advance on invoices that qualify. This helps with cash flow. Helps pay for everyday running costs.

For businesses feeling the squeeze on cash flow, invoice finance can give access to working capital without leaning on traditional business loans or property-backed security.

What Is Invoice Finance?

Invoice finance is a kind of business funding that lets companies borrow against or get an advance on, customer invoices that're still outstanding.

When a business supplies goods or services to another business, it usually sends an invoice with payment terms of 30, 60 or 90 days.

The sale is done.. The business has to wait for payment before it can touch that money.

Invoice finance fills this gap by letting businesses access a percentage of the invoice value sooner.

Put simply, invoice finance turns invoices into working capital.

How Does Invoice Finance Work? (Step by Step)

The invoice finance process is fairly simple once your funding facility has been approved.

Here is how it works with Zapfunds.

Step 1 – Submit Your Invoices

After you supply goods or services to your customers, you send your invoices.

When you need working capital, you submit eligible outstanding invoices through the Zapfunds online portal.

You can pick the invoices you want to finance, subject to your facility agreement and eligibility requirements.

Step 2 – Receive an Advance

Once your invoices have been reviewed and approved, Zapfunds can advance up to 85% of the invoice value, subject to approval and facility terms.

Depending on the facility and verification requirements, funding may be available within 24 hours.

This gives your business access to working capital without waiting for customers to pay.

You can use the funds to:

  • Pay employee wages and subcontractors.
  • Purchase materials and stock.
  • Cover operating expenses.
  • Fund contracts and projects.
  • Manage supplier payments.
  • Support business expansion.

Step 3 – Your Customer Pays the Invoice

Your customer keeps going through their payment process.

Depending on whether your facility operates as invoice factoring or invoice discounting, payments may be collected by the finance provider. Managed by your business.

The customer payment is then applied to the invoice finance balance.

Step 4 – Receive the Remaining Balance

Once your customer pays the invoice, the transaction is reconciled.

The remaining invoice balance is released to your business after deducting the advance and applicable fees.

This completes the invoice finance transaction.

What Are the Different Types of Invoice Finance?

There are two types of invoice finance available to Australian businesses: invoice factoring and invoice discounting.

What Is Invoice Factoring?

Invoice factoring is a funding arrangement where a business sells or assigns its invoices to a finance provider.

The finance provider generally manages the collection process. Receives payment directly from customers.

Invoice factoring can suit businesses that:

  • Want to outsource accounts management.
  • Have limited internal administration resources.
  • Want to reduce the time spent chasing invoices.
  • Need working capital.

Customers are generally aware of the arrangement because payments are directed to the finance provider.

What Is Invoice Discounting?

Invoice discounting allows businesses to access funding against invoices while generally retaining responsibility for customer collections.

The business continues managing its accounts customer relationships.

Invoice discounting can suit established businesses that:

  • Have an existing accounts team.
  • Prefer to manage customer relationships
  • Want greater confidentiality around their funding arrangements.
  • Have business-to-business invoicing.

The availability of confidential invoice discounting depends on the provider and facility agreement.

What Is the Difference Between Invoice Finance and a Business Loan?

Invoice finance and traditional business loans both provide access to working capital,. They work in different ways.

Invoice finance can be especially useful for businesses that are growing, because more sales can open up funding.

However, the best option depends on your business circumstances, funding requirements and the cost of each facility.

How Much Does Invoice Finance Cost in Australia?

Invoice finance is not money. Providers generally charge fees for giving access to working capital and for running the facility.

Common costs can include:

1. Service or discount fee

A fee tied to the funding provided. It may depend on the amount the funding period and the facility structure.

2. Administration fee

A fee that covers the administration and management of the facility.

3. Establishment fee

An fee that may apply when the funding facility is set up.

The actual cost depends on things like your business turnover, invoice quality, customer payment history, industry and facility requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions About Invoice Finance

1. How quickly can I receive invoice finance?

Depending on the provider, facility approval and invoice verification requirements, funding may be available within 24 hours. Existing approved facilities can provide access to working capital.

2. How much of an invoice can I borrow against?

Some invoice finance providers offer advances of up to 85% of an invoices value. The actual advance rate depends on the provider, invoice eligibility and facility terms.

3. Do I need property security for invoice finance?

Many invoice finance facilities do not require commercial property as security. However, providers may require security over business assets, receivables or other guarantees depending on the facility.

4. Does my customer know I am using invoice finance?

It depends on the type of facility. Invoice factoring generally involves customer notification, while confidential invoice discounting may allow your business to manage collections without disclosing the arrangement.

5. Can small businesses use invoice finance?

Yes. Medium-sized businesses may be eligible if they issue qualifying business-to-business invoices and meet the providers assessment requirements.

6. Can I finance invoices?

Depending on your facility, you may be able to finance invoices or a broader portion of your eligible sales ledger.

7. What happens if my customer doesn't pay?

The consequences depend on whether the facility's recourse or non-recourse and the terms of your agreement. Under a recourse arrangement, your business may remain responsible for invoices.

8. Is invoice finance better than a business overdraft?

Neither option is automatically better. Invoice finance is linked to invoices and may grow with eligible sales, while an overdraft provides access to an agreed revolving credit limit. The right option depends on your cash flow requirements and funding costs.

9. Can invoice finance help my business grow?

Yes. By releasing cash tied up in invoices, businesses may have more working capital available to take on new contracts, purchase stock, pay suppliers and manage increasing operating expenses.
