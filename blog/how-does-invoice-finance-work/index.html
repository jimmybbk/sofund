How Does Invoice Finance Work in Australia? A Step-by-Step Guide for Businesses

Zap Funds | 19 November 2026 | 6 min read

You deliver. You invoice. But your payment is held in that customer bank account for 30, 60 or 90 days while the rest of the country pays their suppliers, pays their bills, pays their staff on time and pays them their tax.

This problem can be solved by invoice finance, the finance provider lends you the majority of the invoice value (or your entire value if you chose), collects from your customer and pays you the rest. This guide explains how invoice finance works in Australia and the costs and whether invoice finance could work for you or not.

What is invoice finance?

Invoice finance (sometimes known as debtor finance or also receivables finance) is the provision of a loan secured against your unpaid invoices. The invoice finance company lends you up to 90% of the face value of the invoice to you with an agreement that they will pay the outstanding balance (with charges) when the bill is paid to you.

One security is usually sufficient (the invoice) and the funding is based on your sales, so the more you invoice the more funds you can use.

How invoice finance works, step by step

Step 1: Complete the work

You supply goods or services to a business customer and have agreed payment terms for example 30, 60 or 90 days.

Step 2: Issue the invoice

You issue a proper invoice /progress claim (under the security of payment legislation in your state), having done the work. It will be important at a later date that this is served, so that your supplier can consider it.

Step 3: Submit the invoice for funding

You will pass the invoice plus the attached documents (such as purchase orders, contracts, certificates of delivery or delivery slips) to your financial organisation, usually by an electronic gateway.

Step 4: The provider assesses it

This is where your supplier will check whether the job was carried out by you, whether the invoice is bona fide and they also check if your customer is creditworthy. This is when the fees and advance rate will be determined.

Step 5: You receive the advance

Your supplier releases 90% of the total value of your invoice to you upon approval (usually within 24hrs). Your money is now free to be spent on your team, the next project, taxes, materials etc.

Step 6: Your customer pays the invoice

They either pay you for them or the company they come to you for also provides you with the service.

Step 7: You receive the balance

Your provider will deduct its charges and pay the difference to you; this will constitute the conclusion of your agreement and the process can repeat whenever you make an invoice.

What it looks like in dollars

Consider the following example. A recruitment agency provides a client with five temps and invoices $60,000 for a 30-day period with its temps being paid weekly and being able to get 80% of the total invoice value.

In other words £48,000 available this week - not a month from now. The interest and other charges will vary between services, that is exactly why a comparison needs looking at.

The two main types of invoice finance

Invoice factoring

The collections pass to the new creditors and you can notify your clients and deal with the debt at source. This service is ideal for businesses that do not have to deal with their own credit control and chasing, allowing them not to have to do it themselves.

Invoice discounting

Confidential: your clients continue to make payments to you as usual, the finance company can be kept secret. Suits established companies with a credit control operation in place.

Selective invoice finance

Pay one or even just a couple of invoices at a time, useful if you have a one-time cash flow gap because of a large order. Some suppliers do not allow this, so check.

What does invoice finance cost?

In general, there are three different types of cost providers are asking to charge.

Discount or funding fee: - this is the main cost which depends on the amount being funded and how long you are going to take payment on your invoice.

Administration fee- the cost of running the centre and dealing with all the correspondence

Establishment fee: a one-off charge when the facility is set up.

Your sales, the quality of the customer and the time taken to pay invoices can all make a difference. Always ask for a written estimate and who charges you if the customer takes longer than usual to pay.

Invoice finance or a business loan?

They help with different things. A loan gives you a known amount to use for a larger purchase and fixed repayments for something that will remain over time, e.g., equipment. Whereas invoice finance allows you to get a cash boost for money that is already in your account and allows for expansion as your invoicing increases.

It is not necessarily one or the other, however - it depends whether you are struggling with how much money you have or when. A lot of businesses use both simultaneously.

Who qualifies for invoice finance?

You would invoice other companies on credit, for genuine work done, where the debt was undisputed and your client was credit worthy, be they small tradesmen, construction and trades, transport and logistics, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, recruitment and staffing, cleaning/health and safety professionals, engineering and professional services.

However, to identify which industries are most affected, we need to consider those which have the longest payment chains (i.e. most subcontracting) since these are the sectors where the problem is likely to be the worst. For example, in the year to June 2025, over 25 per cent of company failures in Australia were related to the construction industry - a sector which is heavily subcontracted. Frequently asked questions

How quickly can I get money against an invoice?

Once you have obtained your initial approval from the finance provider (you usually receive this once they have assessed your business and your customers) you can usually access working capital on eligible invoices within 24 hours.

How much of an invoice can I access?

The majority of the finance companies pay out between 80% and 90% of your eligible invoice value so as your sales ledger develops (in some instances even up to a few million) you will have increasing finance available to you.

Do I need property security?

Normally not, but the invoice would be the security for the work. Some companies can require guarantees or charges of assets, so make sure you receive a clear letter of instructions as to the precise requirements.

Will my customers know?

Also you may not want people to know about it, so invoice discounting is normally done under wraps and the customers still pay you, whereas with invoice factoring you would have to let your customers know about it as your supplier handles collections.

Can small businesses qualify?

Yes, and in some cases the size of the business is irrelevant because if the invoice is accurate and not disputed and to a business customer then it does not matter whether it is a small shopkeeper selling bread or a multi-national organisation.

What happens if my customer does not pay?

It all depends on whether it is recourse. With a recourse facility, then the risk is on your business. If it is non-recourse, it will incur extra cost and will have conditions as you are taking on the customer's credit risk.
