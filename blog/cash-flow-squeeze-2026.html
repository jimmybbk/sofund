The 2026 cash flow squeeze.

5 November 2026 · 6 min read · Zap Funds

Here's the strange shape of Australian small business cash flow in 2026: payment times are slowly getting better, yet more businesses than ever are in serious trouble with the tax office. Both things are true at once — and together they explain why so many owners feel squeezed even as the headline numbers improve.

What the payment data actually says

According to Xero's Small Business Insights, small businesses were paid an average of 6.0 days late in the June 2026 quarter — a gradual improvement on earlier quarters, and industry cash flow trackers put the average wait at roughly 23 days by October, down from about 24 in March. The Payment Times Reporting Regulator's August update found 69.8% of invoices from small businesses are now paid within 30 days, the best result since the scheme began.

So progress is real. But "paid 6 days late" still means a business on 60-day terms waits more than two months for money it has already earned. For a payroll-heavy business, that gap is the whole ball game.

The tax twist nobody budgeted for

The bigger story of 2026 is on the other side of the ledger. The Australian Financial Review reported in October that the number of businesses failing to pay tax on time surged 21% to more than 37,000 firms, as the ATO resumed firmer collection after years of leniency. The RBA's October Financial Stability Review flagged the same squeeze: profitability pressure plus tougher debt collection is hitting exactly the small businesses that spent the last few years quietly running on tax debt.

Put simply: the banks' patience got tighter at the same moment the tax office's did. Businesses that used the ATO as an interest-free overdraft for half a decade are now being asked to settle — in the same year their customers are still paying slow.

The escape routes that actually work

  • Fix the timing, not just the total. A loan adds money and adds repayments on a schedule. Invoice finance releases cash you've already earned, timed to when you need it.
  • Deal with ATO debt early, from a position of structure. Lenders in our panel have specific facilities for consolidating tax debt — and a written offer beats a payment plan panic.
  • Stop funding your customers for free. Every 60-day term you extend is an interest-free loan you didn't choose to make. Price it in, or bridge it with invoice finance.
  • Get options in writing before you commit. Confirmed rates and repayments, side by side, are the only way to compare like for like.
