Invoice Finance Facility

Zap Funds | 7 October 2026 | 5 min read

You are offering as security for the facility the value of the outstanding invoices from your customers, so the facility is known as 'Invoice finance'. It is not a loan and it is not an overdraft; it is a limit which is drawn against your debtor ledger meaning that as soon as invoicing occurs you can draw money without having to wait for the invoice to be paid by your customer (30, 60 or 90 days later etc).

In Australia, companies are often on payment terms which make this whole problem exist because they have paid for stock, wage employees etc, completed the job but then have to wait on another company to finally release the money and a facility can help you get this money in advance.

What is an invoice finance facility?

Invoice finance is an arrangement made with your financing house whereby invoices you raise have your customers' creditworthiness considered, your invoices assessed and a limit granted based on the overall value of your invoices. You can then withdraw up to 90% against eligible invoices. The finance available re-instated once the invoices are collected.

It is not a loan against your property/stock; the invoice itself is your security and the limit you have on the account is based against your sales ledger, so as your business expands, so does this facility and you cannot really argue with a loan which is rising to match your business expansion.

How a facility works

If the vendor has been accepted, the following would happen to invoices:

  1. You do work for a company, you invoice them and agree payment terms.
  2. You then hand the invoice, which in most cases has been issued to the site through an internet portal system and evidence of delivery to the venue.
  3. Once your service/ supplier has examined the bill and client, they will issue 90% maximum of the amount shown on the invoice.
  4. The funds are usually credited in your account within 24 hours.
  5. Your customer pays the invoice as normal.
  6. After taking their cut, they pay the balance back to you.
  7. Your money gets added back in so you can spend it again on the next invoice.

A worked example

Say this was a recruitment organisation, on a contract to supply a temporary workforce to an organisation for a client. It will pay its temporary workers each week but the client will take 60 days to pay it. Last month they will have been invoiced for $60,000 to cover this and the payroll is yet to be processed.

What does a facility cost?

However, the lender does not charge interest as a bank would, but instead charges fees:

Discount or funding fee - usually the most expensive part, depending on the value of the invoice and the amount of funding.

Administration fee - the costs of maintaining this service and processing your invoices.

Setting charge: the one-off fee paid to install the machine

The amount may vary depending on your sales, your customer base and your credit control. You must always request this in the letter and the letter should state clearly what happens should a customer pay late.

What happens if a customer pays late?

Facilities are built with the understanding that payment for the invoice may be delayed. If the payment is still not received by its due date, the interest will continue to accrue, so keeping on top of your debtor list and chasing payment is important. In the unlikely event that your client does not pay or if they challenge the invoice they will need to be covered if it is non recourse, whereas recourse (the default) means that you will have to cover the amount. The provider should tell you whether disputes, insolvencies and part payments are covered.

Whole ledger or selective

The whole ledger option allows any or all of your qualified invoices to be financed. This option is best for businesses that prefer a continuous cash-flow and a select option is ideal for those that need to only finance a few or one invoice at a time than keep a constant gap in cash-flow.

Finally, you will decide upon what happens to your customers. With the option invoice factoring, they will be dealing with the company we are supplying on a direct basis and receiving all the invoices from. With the other, confidential invoice discounting option, your customers never get to hear of the facility, and the invoices will come through to you in the normal business manner. We have articles explaining both these facilities in more detail.

Who a facility suits

It can be used by any organisation which charges other organisations, these are the ones that we regularly see:

  • Construction and labour working awaiting progress claims and funds from main contractor
  • Transport including fuel & crews etc that have been paid long before the freight invoice is even paid
  • Purchases in anticipation for a big order from a manufacturer.
  • Hiring companies who pay their weeklies on a monthly basis for the client to work with
  • Wholesaler has to be building up reserves to service retailers that are slow payers.
  • IT Contractors and Consultancies charging by project on a 30 to 60 day basis

The message is the same however: money is owed and not paid in time and yet work done and invoiced is profitable work!

How much can you get

It is not how much money the business has in a bank account, but the amount of the business's ledger. So a business that invoices $250,000 a month with a good customer base may well have a limit of greater than $200,000. Even if the business is relatively new, it can still be a factor as there is more of a factor of customer quality than just trading history as a consideration. As the invoice is raised the limit increases - and is paid down as it is collected. For many, especially new businesses it is regarded as a second bank account built through their sales.

What to compare before you sign

As a customer, you will find that providers have different facilities than many believe (before they have signed anything). Get this in black and white.

  • The capacity (maximum number of transactions or invoices) available and its scale as you invoice more
  • What % the advance you get and the total amount they pay after 30, 60 or 90 days
  • Whether or not this facility is recourse/non recourse (i.e. what happens if the customer does not pay)
  • Whether your customers are informed or not (keep this a secret or not)
  • All other applicable charges such as exit fees/min usage

A broker comparison means that you receive exactly the same data from a selection of lenders, formatted and presented in the same way.

Frequently asked questions

How is a facility different from a business loan?

A loan is an agreed sum which has regular monthly payments, perfect for one-time large purchases. A facility is your credit account which works with your invoicing and which gets paid when your customers settle their accounts.

How much can I draw against an invoice?

This is usually between 80 and 90% of the eligible value of the invoice.

How fast is the first funding?

After your credit limit has been initially verified and approved, this will normally occur in one day. You will usually receive payment for the majority of new invoices within a couple of days.

Do I have to finance every invoice?

There is the whole ledger where any invoices that are eligible are eligible for funding and individual facilities, which allow funding of one invoice at a time when you need it.

Do I need property security?

Typically not. Invoices are the security although in some cases guarantees or charges may be taken in return for company assets.

Does invoice finance work with ATO debt?

It may well do; when a business is under pressure from the ATO, the company may find a service that allows it pay down its debt and settle up into the regular repayment scheme using the account as collateral. Not all offer these services from both a financial and practical point, so it is worth checking at the outset.

Can I keep my bank relationship?

Yes, they often go hand in hand with your bank accounts and any loans you may have. The only thing you will need to establish is that your bank does not have a charge over your receivables or they will not lend you money as they will not be able to provide a loan.

The bottom line

If you are waiting on some customers to pay you after you issue an invoice for 30, 60 or 90 days, then it is probably worth your while considering putting in to check on an invoice finance facility. Not only should it help to ease your cash flow, you do not need to raise any debt on a balance sheet or put any collateral (such as property) up, nor would it be affected in a downturn. It is a straightforward process that will release funds that have actually been earned, put them straight back in to your account at a time when it matters most, all the while enabling you to finance future jobs without waiting for anyone else to decide what is acceptable. In the long run, your company will be able to accept large contracts, pay its staff regularly and expand according to the market's demands.
