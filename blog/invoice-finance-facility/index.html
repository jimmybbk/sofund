What is an invoice finance facility?

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

An invoice finance facility is not a loan. It is a running funding line secured against the invoices your customers have not paid yet. You do the work, you issue the invoice, and the facility lets you draw most of that invoice's value immediately instead of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days for your customer to get around to it.

That single difference, money when the work is done rather than money when the customer feels like paying, is why thousands of Australian businesses run their cash flow through an invoice finance facility instead of leaning on loans, overdrafts or credit cards.

How a facility works once it is set up

When you first apply, the finance provider assesses your business, your customers and your invoicing, then approves a facility limit. From that point the cycle is simple:

  1. You complete work for a business customer and issue an invoice.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal.
  3. Up to 90% of the invoice value is paid into your account, often within 24 hours.
  4. Your customer pays the invoice on their normal terms.
  5. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.
  6. The available funds reset, ready for your next invoice.

There is no fixed repayment schedule fighting your cash flow, because the advance is settled by your customer's payment. And when your business grows and you invoice more, the funding grows with it.

What it costs

Providers charge fees, not interest in the loan sense. The three you will normally see:

  • Discount or funding fee: the main cost, based on the amount drawn and how long the invoice takes to pay.
  • Administration fee: for running the facility and processing invoices.
  • Establishment fee: a one-off charge when the facility is first set up.

The exact pricing depends on your sales, the credit quality of your customers and how long your invoices stay outstanding. Always ask for the full cost breakdown in writing, and ask what happens to the fees if a customer pays late.

Whole ledger or selective facilities

Whole ledger facilities cover every eligible invoice you issue. They suit businesses that want a permanent funding line working quietly in the background.

Selective facilities let you fund single invoices, or a small group, only when you need the cash. They suit businesses with occasional big contracts or seasonal gaps that do not want a permanent arrangement.

Within both structures you will also choose between invoice factoring, where the provider manages the collections, and confidential invoice discounting, where your customers keep paying you directly. Our guides cover both in detail.

Who a facility suits

An invoice finance facility fits any business that invoices other businesses on payment terms. The patterns we see most:

  • Construction, trades and civil contractors waiting on progress claims and head contractor payments
  • Transport and freight operators covering fuel and wages long before the freight invoice pays
  • Manufacturers buying materials ahead of large orders
  • Recruitment and staffing agencies paying weekly workers on monthly client terms
  • Wholesalers stocking up for retailers who pay slowly
  • IT contractors and consultancies billing 30 to 60 day engagements

The common thread: profitable work, done and invoiced, with the cash stuck on the other side of a payment term.

What to compare before you sign

Facilities differ more between providers than most people expect. Before signing anything, get these in writing:

  • The facility limit and how quickly it grows as you invoice more
  • The advance percentage per invoice, and the total cost if your customer pays at 30, 60 or 90 days
  • Whether the facility is recourse or non recourse, and what happens if a customer does not pay
  • Whether customers are notified, or the arrangement stays confidential
  • Every fee that applies, including exit fees and charges for minimum usage

Invoice finance facility or a business loan?

A loan is the right tool when you are buying something long lived: equipment, vehicles, a fit-out. You know the amount, the asset justifies the cost, and fixed repayments match the asset's life.

A facility is the right tool when the problem is timing. You have earned the money, your customer has not paid yet, and payroll does not care. It is also the better fit when you do not want property security hanging over the business, or when your sales are growing and a fixed loan would cap you.

Plenty of businesses run both: a loan for the big purchase, the facility for the working capital.

Frequently asked questions

How is a facility different from a loan?

A loan is a fixed amount with fixed repayments. A facility is a running limit against your invoices: every eligible invoice you issue increases what you can draw, and each advance is settled when your customer pays.

How much can I draw against an invoice?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of an eligible invoice's value. The exact rate depends on the provider, your customer's creditworthiness and the facility terms.

How fast is the first funding?

The first approval takes the longest because the provider assesses your business, your invoices and your customers. After the facility is live, advances against new invoices are typically funded within 24 hours.

Do I have to finance every invoice?

Not always. Whole ledger facilities cover all eligible invoices. Selective facilities let you fund single invoices when you need cash. Ask which structure the provider offers before signing.

Do I need property security?

Generally no. The invoices are the security. Some providers may take guarantees or charges over business assets, so get the requirements in writing before signing.
