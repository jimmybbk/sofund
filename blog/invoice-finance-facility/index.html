Invoice Finance Facility

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

An invoice finance facility is the term the finance industry uses for a running funding line secured against your unpaid invoices. It is not a loan in the traditional sense, and it is not an overdraft. It is an agreed limit that sits against your debtor ledger and lets you draw cash as you issue invoices, instead of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days for your customers to pay.

For Australian businesses with long payment terms, that gap is the whole problem. You have paid for materials, you have paid wages, you have finished the work, and the money for it will not arrive until your customer decides it is their turn to pay. A facility moves that money forward.

What is an invoice finance facility?

An invoice finance facility is an agreement between your business and a finance provider. The provider approves a limit, usually based on the value of your invoices and the creditworthiness of your customers. When you issue an eligible invoice, you can draw up to 90% of its value from the facility. When your customer pays, you receive the remaining balance and the available funds reset.

You are not borrowing against your house or your equipment. The invoice itself is the security. And because the limit follows your sales ledger, the facility grows as your business grows, which is something no fixed loan can do.

How a facility works

Once the facility is approved, the cycle for each invoice looks like this:

  1. You complete the work for a business customer and issue a tax invoice with your agreed payment terms.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal, along with proof of delivery.
  3. The provider assesses the invoice and your customer, then advances you up to 90% of the invoice value.
  4. The money lands in your account, often within 24 hours of submission.
  5. Your customer pays the invoice on their normal terms.
  6. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.
  7. Your available funds reset, ready for the next invoice.

A worked example

Imagine a recruitment agency that places temporary staff with a client. It pays its workers every week, but the client pays on 60 day terms. Last month the agency invoiced $60,000 for the placement, and the payroll still has to run.

ItemAmount
Invoice value$60,000
Advance at 80%$48,000 within 24 hours
Balance held until the customer pays$12,000
Customer pays on day 60$60,000
Balance released to you, minus fees$12,000

That is $48,000 in the bank this week instead of in two months. Exact rates and fees vary between providers, which is why comparing written offers matters.

What does a facility cost?

Providers do not charge interest the way a bank does. The costs come as fees:

  • Discount or funding fee: the main cost, based on the amount drawn and how long the invoice takes to pay.
  • Administration fee: covers the running of the facility and the processing of your invoices.
  • Establishment fee: a one off charge when the facility is first set up.

The total depends on your sales, the quality of your customers and how long your invoices stay outstanding. Always ask for the full breakdown in writing, and always ask what happens to the fees if a customer pays late.

What happens if a customer pays late?

Facilities are built around the reality that customers pay late, but fees do not stop. If an invoice runs past its due date, the funding fee keeps accruing for the extra days, so it pays to stay on top of your debtor list and chase payment alongside the provider. If a customer disputes the invoice or fails entirely, what happens next depends on whether the facility is recourse or non recourse. Ask the provider exactly how disputes, insolvencies and part payments are handled before you sign anything.

Whole ledger or selective

A whole ledger facility covers every eligible invoice you issue and suits businesses that want funding running in the background at all times. A selective facility lets you fund single invoices, or a small group, only when you need them. It suits businesses with occasional large contracts rather than a permanent gap.

Within either structure you will also choose how your customers are handled. With invoice factoring the provider manages the collections and your customers pay them directly. With confidential invoice discounting your customers keep paying you and usually never know the facility exists. Our guides on invoice factoring and debtor finance cover both in detail.

Who a facility suits

Any business that invoices other businesses on payment terms can use one. The patterns we see most often:

  • Construction and trades, waiting on progress claims and head contractor payments
  • Transport and freight, covering fuel and wages long before the freight invoice pays
  • Manufacturers buying materials ahead of large orders
  • Recruitment agencies paying weekly workers on monthly client terms
  • Wholesalers stocking up for retailers who pay slowly
  • IT contractors and consultancies billing 30 to 60 day engagements

The common thread is profitable work, done and invoiced, with the cash on the wrong side of a payment term.

How much can you get

Limits are set against your ledger, not your assets. A business invoicing $250,000 a month with strong customers might carry a limit well above $200,000, while a newer business can still qualify because the assessment leans on customer quality more than trading history. As invoices are raised and paid, the available funds move up and down within the limit, which is why many owners treat the facility as a second bank account built from sales.

What to compare before you sign

Facilities differ more between providers than most owners expect. Before signing anything, get these in writing:

  • The facility limit and how quickly it grows as you invoice more
  • The advance percentage, and the total cost if your customer pays at 30, 60 or 90 days
  • Whether the facility is recourse or non recourse, and what happens if a customer does not pay
  • Whether your customers are notified, or the arrangement stays confidential
  • Every fee that applies, including exit fees and minimum usage charges

This is what a broker comparison gives you: the same numbers, in the same format, from multiple lenders.

Frequently asked questions

How is a facility different from a business loan?

A loan is a fixed amount with fixed repayments, suited to long lived purchases. A facility is a running limit against your invoices, settled when your customers pay.

How much can I draw against an invoice?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of an eligible invoice value.

How fast is the first funding?

The first approval takes the longest because the provider assesses your business and your customers. After that, advances against new invoices are typically funded within 24 hours.

Do I have to finance every invoice?

No. Whole ledger facilities cover all eligible invoices, while selective facilities let you fund single invoices when you need cash.

Do I need property security?

Generally no. The invoices are the security, though some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets.

Does invoice finance work with ATO debt?

It can. Businesses under ATO pressure often use a facility to clear tax debt and bring it into a normal repayment pattern, using their invoices as the security. Not every provider funds ATO arrangements, so confirm this early.

Can I keep my bank relationship?

Yes. Facilities commonly run alongside existing bank accounts and loans. The one thing to check is whether your bank holds a charge over your receivables, since that can affect the provider position.

The bottom line

If your business invoices other businesses on 30, 60 or 90 day terms and the wait is hurting, an invoice finance facility is worth pricing. It does not add debt in the traditional sense, it does not take your property, and it grows with your sales. What it does is simple: it moves the money you have already earned into your account while the work is still fresh in everyones mind, and it keeps the next job funded without waiting on anyone else approval. The end result is a business that can take the next big contract with total confidence, pay its people on time, and grow at the pace the wider market allows.
