Invoice Funding

Zap Funds | 8 October 2026 | 5 min read

You have done the work. The invoice has been sent. And now the money your business has already earned sits in your customer's bank account for another 30, 60 or 90 days while yours struggles on. Invoice funding fixes that: it turns the invoices you have issued into cash you can use now.

What is invoice funding?

Invoice funding means getting cash against invoices your customers have not paid yet. A finance provider advances you most of the invoice value, often within 24 hours of it being issued, and collects the balance and fees when your customer settles up.

It is not a loan against your house or your equipment. The invoice itself is the security, which is why businesses without property can qualify, and why the funding grows automatically as you invoice more.

How invoice funding works in five steps

  1. You complete work for a business customer and issue an invoice on your normal terms.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal, with proof of delivery.
  3. The provider checks the invoice and your customer, then advances up to 90% of its value, often within 24 hours.
  4. Your customer pays the invoice on their normal terms.
  5. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.

Three ways to get invoice funding

Invoice factoring

The provider manages the collections: your customers are notified and pay the provider directly. Suits businesses that want chasing handled for them. Read our full guide to invoice factoring.

Invoice discounting

Confidential: your customers keep paying you directly and usually never know a finance company is involved. Suits established businesses with their own credit control.

Selective invoice funding

Fund single invoices, or a small group, only when you need the cash. Suits businesses with occasional large contracts rather than a permanent funding line.

What it looks like in dollars

A transport company delivers a freight run and invoices $45,000 on 60 day terms. Fuel and wages for that run are due this week. With an 85% advance rate:

ItemAmount
Invoice value$45,000
Funding at 85%$38,250 within 24 hours
Balance until the customer pays$6,750
Customer pays on day 60$45,000
Balance released to you, minus fees$6,750

$38,250 this week for work already delivered, and the facility resets for the next run.

Who gets invoice funding

Any business that invoices other businesses on payment terms. We see it most in transport and freight, recruitment and staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, cleaning and facilities, IT and professional services, engineering and trade services and construction.

Frequently asked questions

What is invoice funding?

Invoice funding means getting cash against invoices your customers have not paid yet. A finance provider advances most of the invoice value, and collects the balance and fees when your customer pays.

How much of an invoice can be funded?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of an eligible invoice's value, with the balance released when your customer pays, minus fees.

Does invoice funding work for small businesses?

Yes. Size matters less than the invoice: genuine, undisputed invoices to business customers are what providers assess.

Does invoice funding need property security?

Generally no. The invoices act as the security, though some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets.
