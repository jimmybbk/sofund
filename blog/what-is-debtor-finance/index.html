What is debtor finance?

Zap Funds | 17 July 2026 | 6 min read

Debtor finance is a type of business funding that turns your unpaid customer invoices into working capital you can use today. Instead of waiting 30, 60 or 90 days for your customers to pay, a finance provider advances you up to 90% of the invoice value, usually within 24 hours of it being issued, and releases the remaining balance when your customer settles.

It goes by several names: invoice finance, invoice factoring, invoice discounting and receivables finance all describe funding secured against your debtor ledger. Whatever the label, the mechanics are the same: the invoice is the security, the advance is settled when your customer pays, and the funding grows as your invoicing grows.

Why businesses use debtor finance

The gap between completing work and getting paid is where good businesses get into trouble. Wages, materials, fuel, rent and tax all fall due on their own schedule, regardless of when your customers decide to pay. Debtor finance converts those waiting invoices into working capital, so payroll and suppliers are covered while the customer pays on their terms.

It also removes the need to turn down work. Businesses on tight cash flow decline bigger contracts for a simple reason: the bigger the job, the bigger the upfront costs. With funding against the invoices, larger contracts stop being a risk and start being growth.

How debtor finance works

The cycle for each invoice is short once a facility is approved:

  1. You complete work for a business customer and issue an invoice with agreed payment terms.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal.
  3. The provider verifies the invoice and your customer, then advances up to 90% of its value, often within 24 hours.
  4. Your customer pays the invoice on their normal terms.
  5. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.
  6. The available funds reset, ready for the next invoice.

There are no fixed repayments fighting your cash flow between cycles, because the advance is settled by your customer's payment rather than by a schedule.

The two main types of debtor finance

Invoice factoring

The provider manages the collections: your customers are notified and pay the provider directly. Suits businesses that want chasing handled for them, or that operate without a dedicated accounts team.

Invoice discounting

Confidential: your customers keep paying you directly as normal and usually never know a finance company is involved. Suits established businesses with their own credit control.

Within both types you can also choose a whole ledger facility, where every eligible invoice flows through automatically, or a selective facility that funds single invoices when you need cash. Read more in our guide to invoice factoring.

A worked example

Imagine a recruitment agency that places temporary staff with a client. It pays its workers every week, but the client pays on 30 day terms. The agency invoices $50,000 for the month:

ItemAmount
Invoice value$50,000
Advance at 85%$42,500 within 24 hours
Balance until the client pays$7,500
Client pays on day 30$50,000
Balance released to you, minus fees$7,500

$42,500 this week, so payroll runs and the agency can keep placing.

What debtor finance costs

Providers charge fees rather than interest. The main cost is the discount or funding fee, calculated on the amount advanced and how long the invoice stays outstanding. Administration fees cover the running of the facility, and a one off establishment fee may apply when it is set up. The total depends on your sales, the quality of your customers and how long your invoices stay unpaid.

Always ask for the full cost breakdown in writing, and always ask what happens to the fees if a customer pays late.

Who debtor finance suits

Any business invoicing other businesses on payment terms. The industries we see most are transport and freight, recruitment and staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, cleaning and facilities, IT and professional services, engineering and trade services, and construction and trades.

Frequently asked questions about debtor finance

Is debtor finance the same as invoice finance?

Yes. Debtor finance, invoice finance, factoring and receivables finance are different names for funding secured against your unpaid customer invoices.

How much of an invoice can I access?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of an eligible invoice's value, with the balance released when your customer pays, minus fees.

Do my customers need to know?

Not with confidential arrangements. Invoice discounting keeps your customers paying you directly, while factoring involves notifying them.

Does debtor finance require property security?

Generally no. The invoices act as the security, though some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets.

Can I fund single invoices instead of everything?

Yes. Selective facilities fund the invoices you choose, which suits one off contracts and seasonal gaps.
