Invoice factoring

Invoice factoring,
made simple.

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

Invoice factoring is the most hands off form of invoice finance. You sell your unpaid invoices to a finance provider, receive up to 90% of their value within 24 hours, and the provider manages the collections from your customers. When they pay, you receive the remaining balance minus the provider's fees.

For businesses without a dedicated accounts team, factoring effectively outsources credit control AND funds the work at the same time. No more Sunday nights spent chasing 60 day invoices.

How invoice factoring works

Once a factoring facility is approved, the cycle for each invoice is straightforward:

  1. You complete work for a business customer and issue a valid invoice.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal.
  3. The provider verifies the invoice and your customer, then advances up to 90% of its value, often within 24 hours.
  4. Your customer pays the invoice to the provider on their normal terms.
  5. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.

The facility grows with your sales: every new invoice increases the funding available, so growth stops being a cash flow gamble.

What factoring costs

Factoring providers charge fees rather than interest. The main cost is the discount or funding fee, calculated on the amount advanced and how long the invoice takes to pay. On top of that you may see an administration fee for managing the collections and a one off establishment fee when the facility is set up.

The total cost depends on your sales volume, the credit quality of your customers and how long your invoices stay outstanding. Always ask for the complete cost breakdown in writing, and always ask what happens to fees when a customer pays late.

Factoring vs invoice discounting

Both products fund the same invoices; the difference is who manages the collections. Factoring is disclosed: your customers are notified and pay the provider directly, and the chasing is done for you. Discounting is confidential: your customers keep paying you as normal and usually never know a finance company is involved.

Factoring suits businesses that want collections handled. Discounting suits established businesses with their own credit control that value confidentiality. Many providers offer both, so the choice can sit inside a single facility.

Factoring vs a business loan

A business loan is a fixed amount with fixed repayments, assessed on your financials, assets and credit history. Factoring is linked to your invoices, so the funding grows with your sales and the assessment focuses on your receivables and customer risk rather than property security. Neither is automatically better; the right choice depends on whether your problem is the amount of money or the timing of it.

Who uses invoice factoring

Any business invoicing other businesses on payment terms can use factoring. We see it most in transport and freight, recruitment and staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, cleaning and facilities and engineering and trade services.

The common thread: genuine work delivered, invoices issued, and customers who pay on terms. If that is your business, factoring turns your debtor ledger into working capital you can use today.

Common questions about invoice factoring

What is the difference between invoice factoring and invoice discounting?

Factoring is disclosed: the provider manages collections and your customers pay them directly. Discounting is confidential: your customers keep paying you and usually never know a finance company is involved.

How fast can a factored invoice be paid?

Once the facility is approved, advances against new eligible invoices are typically funded within 24 hours of submission.

How much of the invoice does factoring advance?

Most providers advance between 80% and 90% of the invoice value upfront, with the balance released when your customer pays, minus fees.

Do I need property security for invoice factoring?

Generally no. The invoices themselves act as the security, though some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets.

Can small businesses use invoice factoring?

Yes. Size matters less than the invoice: genuine, undisputed invoices to business customers are what providers assess.
