Invoice factoring

Invoice factoring,
made simple.

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

Invoice factoring is one of the more hands-off styles of financing you could expect, and invoice factoring entails selling your invoices onto a third party (a finance provider), obtaining up to 90%. After 24 hours, which they then chase payment off from the customer until they make payment. Upon receipt of payment, the remainder is passed onto you minus the charges made by the provider.

If you do not even have a dedicated accounts team in your business, then factoring is credit control and cash at the same time. No more spending Sunday night chasing up 60-day invoices.

How invoice factoring works

The process applied to each invoice with the approved factoring facility runs as follows.

  1. You complete work for a business customer and issue a valid invoice.
  2. You submit the invoice to the facility, usually through an online portal.
  3. The provider verifies the invoice and your customer, then advances up to 90% of its value, often within 24 hours.
  4. Your customer pays the invoice to the provider on their normal terms.
  5. The provider deducts its fees and releases the remaining balance to you.

Scaling with your success, more invoices mean more funds, and growth becomes a choice, not a gamble.

What factoring costs

Instead of interest, factoring providers charge fees. Chiefly, then, there will be charged a discount or funding fee to account for the amount being advanced and for how long the invoice takes to pay. In addition to these charges, there may be charged an administration fee for arranging them and an establishment fee at the setting up of the credit facility.

The total cost will depend upon how much business you sell; how good the credit repayment is of your customers; and, how long bills are 'out-standing' for that you need funding for. Always have the total break-down costs in writing, also what effect late-paying customers will have on fees.

Factoring vs invoice discounting

The main difference between factoring and discounting products is that customers know how their invoices are going to be funded. Factoring is disclosed so customers know that their invoices are passed over to the funding facility because they are asked to pay the factor directly. Discounting is confidential because customers continue to pay the client they have ordered bills from. Usually, they do not know a funding facility has been created for the client they are ordering bills from.

Factoring is the right choice for businesses that want collections handled. Discounting is suited to established businesses having their own credit control. Also, they prefer confidentiality. Several other sources offer both, thus the choice can be combined within one facility.

Factoring vs a business loan

A business loan is a fixed amount with fixed payment. It is offered against your financials, assets, and credit history. Factoring, on the other hand, is linked to your invoices. This means the funding can grow with your sales, and less focus will be placed on property security, but more focus on your receivables and risks involved in dealing with your customers. There is no better option than the other, and the choice depends on your problem. Is it the amount of money or the flow?

Who uses invoice factoring

Any business invoicing other businesses on payment terms can use factoring. We see it most in transport and freight, recruitment and staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, cleaning and facilities and engineering and trade services.

What have these people all in common? They do some proper work, they raise invoices, and their customers pay to terms. If this is your business, then Factoring turns your debtor ledger into working capital you may use today.

Common questions about invoice factoring

What is the difference between invoice factoring and invoice discounting?

Factor. Your collections are looked after for you and your clients pay directly to the provider. The discount arrangement is fully confidential because the clients carry on paying to you and usually will not know that there is a funding company involved at all.

How fast can a factored invoice be paid?

When the facility has been approved, invoices that are eligible may normally be funded by drawing advances which are paid, according to when the invoices have been uploaded to them, generally within a maximum of 24 hours.

How much of the invoice does factoring advance?

The majority of providers will advance you 80-90% of the invoice value by raising the advanced amounts, leaving the rest on hold. It will be released when your client has paid (minus fees).

Do I need property security for invoice factoring?

Generally, no. Invoices themselves act as a sort of security, although some companies take guarantees or other charges over the business's assets.

Can small businesses use invoice factoring?

Yes. Not size of customer it matters, but invoice: real and undisputed invoices to business customers is what providers evaluate.
