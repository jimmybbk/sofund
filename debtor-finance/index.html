Debtor finance

Debtor finance,
explained.

Debtor finance is an umbrella term for funding secured against your debtor ledger: the money your customers owe you for work already done. Invoice factoring and invoice discounting are both types of debtor finance.

How debtor finance works

A finance provider reviews your ledger and sets a facility limit. When you issue eligible invoices, you can draw up to an agreed percentage of their value, typically around 85%, instead of waiting out 30, 60 or 90 day payment terms. When your customer pays, the facility is repaid and the remaining balance, after fees, is released to you.

Whole turnover or selective

  • Whole turnover debtor finance: all eligible invoices flow through the facility. It suits businesses wanting a permanent, self managing funding line.
  • Selective debtor finance: you choose which invoices to fund, invoice by invoice. It suits businesses that want flexibility, or whose customers include a mix of payment behaviours.

What debtor finance offers

  • Working capital without property security
  • Funding that grows as your sales grow
  • Cash within 24 hours of issuing eligible invoices
  • Confidential or disclosed arrangements
