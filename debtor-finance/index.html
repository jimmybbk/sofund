Debtor finance

Debtor finance,
explained.

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

Debtor Finance is the general term used to describe finance you acquire for your business based on money that is out with your customers, through work already carried out. If your business is one that raises invoices on 30, 60 or 90 days, you will be well versed in having money out with your customers. Debtor Finance changes those invoices into real cash, today.

They are forms of debtor finance; it depends on whether you wish collection activity or not and the level of amount within the ledger itself.

How debtor finance works

Your finance provider will review your ledger and approve your facility limit. As they become ready, eligible invoices will be drawn up against and a limit of x amount (up to c. 90%) may be drawn. All paid amounts will be reduced to what amount is left on the invoices after a facility limit, and the rest after any charges, will be paid back to yourselves.

Grow with sales: the more you invoice, the more funds are available to you, without having to re-apply or re-negotiate.

Whole ledger or selective debtor finance

Whole ledger facilities cover all of your eligible invoices automatically. Every invoice you issue flows through the facility, the funding resets as customers pay, and the administration becomes part of your routine. This suits businesses that want a permanent funding line working in the background.

Selective facilities let you fund single invoices, or a small group, only when you need the cash. This suits businesses with occasional large contracts, seasonal gaps, or a mix of customers where funding everything would be wasteful.

Within both structures you choose between invoice factoring, where the provider manages the collections and your customers are notified, and confidential invoice discounting, where your customers keep paying you directly and usually never know the facility exists.

What debtor finance offers

  • Working capital without property security: the invoices act as the collateral
  • Cash within 24 hours of issuing eligible invoices, once the facility is approved
  • Funding that grows as your sales grow, without reapplying
  • Confidential or disclosed arrangements, depending on what suits your customer relationships
  • The ability to keep your bank lines free for other purposes

Which businesses use debtor finance

Debtor finance is generally relevant to businesses that supply goods or services to other businesses and issue invoices with payment terms. We see it most in transport and freight, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, recruitment and staffing, cleaning and facilities and engineering and trade services.

All have one thing in common though: they all deliver work that is profitable. They produce invoices to a range of companies which will always pay up before point of delivery.

Frequently asked questions about debtor finance

What is the difference between debtor finance and invoice finance?

Debtor finance describes the type of funding that uses your debtor ledger as security. Invoice factoring and invoice discounting are two types of debtor finance funding facilities.

What is the difference between whole ledger and selective debtor finance?

Whole ledger facilities fund all eligible invoices and are designed for businesses looking for a permanent line of funding. Selective facilities allow you to choose single invoices for funding when you are short of cash.

How much of an invoice can debtor finance advance?

Providers advance normally 80-90% of the value of an eligible invoice, with the balance released when your customer pays, minus fees.

Does debtor finance require property security?

For the majority, the answer is no. This is because the invoices themselves provide security, though businesses are known for taking up other guarantees or charges of business assets.
