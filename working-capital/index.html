Working capital

Working capital,
released.

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

Working capital is the money your business has available for daily operations: wages, materials, rent and suppliers. Healthy working capital means you can say yes to work. Tight working capital means every new job feels like a risk.

For business to business operators, the biggest trap is the invoice cycle. You pay for labour and materials now, invoice your customer, then wait 30, 60 or 90 days. Every new contract quietly absorbs more of your cash before it returns a cent.

What ties working capital up

  • Payment terms: business to business invoicing on 30, 60 or 90 days is standard practice.
  • Slow payers: invoices that slip past their due date while your obligations do not move.
  • Growth: winning bigger contracts means bigger upfront costs before the bigger income arrives.
  • ATO pressure: tax obligations run on their own schedule and ignore your invoice dates entirely.
  • Seasonality: lumpy revenue with steady overheads.

How invoice finance releases working capital

An invoice finance facility advances up to 90% of your unpaid invoices within 24 hours of issue, and settles when your customer pays. There are no fixed repayments fighting your cash flow, because the advance is repaid from the invoice itself. And as your sales grow, the facility grows with them.

Compare the options honestly:

  • Business loans: a fixed lump sum with fixed repayments. Useful for long lived assets, but repayments ignore your invoice timing.
  • Overdrafts: a revolving limit secured against property, with limits that do not grow with sales.
  • Invoice finance: funding secured by your invoices, releasing up to 90% of their value within 24 hours, settled when your customer pays. It grows as your sales grow.

Why businesses choose invoice finance for working capital

  • No property security needed in most facilities
  • Available within 24 hours of issuing eligible invoices
  • Funding capacity rises with every new invoice you issue
  • Confidential arrangements available
  • Facilities commonly run alongside existing bank accounts and loans

The industries with the biggest working capital gaps

Working capital pressure shows up wherever invoices pay slower than wages. We see it most in transport and freight, recruitment and staffing, manufacturing, wholesale and distribution, cleaning and facilities, IT and professional services and engineering and trade services.

Frequently asked questions about working capital

What is working capital?

Working capital is the money your business has available for day to day operations: wages, materials, rent and suppliers. Healthy working capital means you can say yes to work.

How does invoice finance release working capital?

It advances up to 90% of your unpaid invoices within 24 hours of issue, settled when your customer pays. The funding grows as your invoicing grows.

Does invoice finance require property security?

Generally no. The invoices act as the security, though some providers take guarantees or charges over business assets.

Can working capital funding run alongside a bank loan?

Often yes. Facilities commonly run alongside existing bank accounts and loans. Check whether your bank holds a charge over your receivables.
