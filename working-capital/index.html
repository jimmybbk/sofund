Working capital

Working capital,
released.

Working capital is the money your business has available for day to day operations: wages, materials, rent, suppliers. Healthy working capital means you can say yes to work. Tight working capital means every new job feels like a risk.

What ties working capital up

For business to business operators, the biggest trap is the invoice cycle. You pay for labour and materials now, invoice your customer, then wait 30, 60 or 90 days. Every new contract quietly absorbs more of your cash before it returns a cent.

The usual options, honestly compared

  • Business loans: a fixed lump sum with fixed repayments. Useful for long lived assets, but repayments ignore your invoice timing.
  • Overdrafts: a revolving limit secured against property, with limits that do not grow with sales.
  • Invoice finance: funding secured by your invoices, releasing up to 85% of their value within 24 hours, settled when your customer pays. It grows as your sales grow.

Why businesses choose invoice finance for working capital

  • No property security needed in most facilities
  • Available within 24 hours of issuing eligible invoices
  • Funding capacity rises with every new invoice you issue
  • Confidential arrangements available
