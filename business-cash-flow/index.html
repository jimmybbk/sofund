Business cash flow

Business cash flow,
fixed at the source.

Cash flow is the number one pressure on Australian small businesses, and most of the time the problem is not profitability. It is timing. You have done the work, issued the invoice, and now your money sits in someone else bank account for 30, 60 or 90 days.

Where cash flow gaps come from

  • Payment terms: business to business invoicing on 30, 60 or 90 days is standard.
  • Slow payers: invoices that slip past their due date while payroll does not.
  • Growth: winning bigger contracts means bigger costs before bigger income.
  • ATO pressure: tax obligations arrive on a schedule that ignores your invoice dates.
  • Seasonality: lumpy revenue with steady overheads.

Why loans often are not the answer

A loan adds a fixed repayment on a fixed date, which can strangle a business through a quiet period. If the underlying problem is timing, the fix is to speed up the money you have already earned rather than borrow money you have not.

How invoice finance fixes the timing

Invoice finance releases up to 85% of an invoice value within 24 hours of it being issued. There is no new debt to service on a schedule: the advance is settled when your customer pays. It is funding that starts from work you have already completed, and it grows as your invoices grow.
