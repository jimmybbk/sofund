Business cash flow

Business cash flow,
fixed at the source.

Zap Funds | 22 November 2026 | 5 min read

The top pressure facing Australian small businesses is cash flow. It is usually timing, not cash, that is the issue. That you have previously done the work, issued bills for it and now your money is sitting somewhere else collecting interest for 30, 60 or 90 days and payroll, suppliers and especially the tax office still insist upon being paid according to the original payment schedule.

Why profitable businesses run out of cash

Cash flow and profit are not the same thing. You might have a healthy margin on your profit and loss statement but an empty bank account because a lot of that revenue in the statement is trapped in unpaid invoices - Xero Small Business Insights reveals the payment time for small businesses averaging 24 days and at least one in ten invoices are unpaid after more than 60 days. This lag time has to be supported by someone - by default, that is you.

The four causes of the cash flow gap

  • Payment terms: business to business invoicing on 30, 60 or 90 days is standard practice.
  • Slow payers: invoices that slip past their due date while your obligations do not move.
  • Growth: winning bigger contracts means bigger upfront costs before the bigger income arrives.
  • ATO pressure: tax obligations run on their own schedule and ignore your invoice dates entirely.

How to fix cash flow at the source

The source of the gap is the invoice waiting to be paid, so the fix releases that money early. An invoice finance facility advances you up to 90% of an eligible invoice within 24 hours of it being issued. The advance is settled when your customer pays, which means there are no fixed loan repayments fighting your cash flow between payment cycles.

That is compared to a loan: a loan adds a fixed repayment on a fixed date which through a quiet month can strangle a business. Invoice finance is about moving around timing already earned money instead of adding new debt.

What businesses use invoice finance cash for

  • Payroll: wages and subcontractors paid on time, every time, regardless of client payment behaviour
  • Suppliers: taking early payment discounts and keeping materials flowing
  • Growth: accepting bigger contracts and hiring the staff to deliver them
  • ATO arrangements: bringing tax debt into a managed position while operations continue

Frequently asked questions about business cash flow

Why do profitable businesses have cash flow problems?

Profit and cash flow are distinct measures. A business making 'profits' might have cash tied up in unpaid invoices from customers for 30, 60, or 90 days.

How fast can invoice finance improve cash flow?

Once the facility's approved advances against eligible invoices would typically be funded within 24 hours of submission.

Does fixing cash flow require taking on debt?

It is different with invoice finance. When your customer pays the invoice, the advance is settled, meaning there are no fixed loan repayments eating your cashflow.

Can invoice finance help with ATO debt pressure?

Absolutely! Many businesses use these sorts of facilities to bring a debt to the ATO under a managed arrangement while covering payroll and suppliers. Specific ATO debt solutions are offered by some providers.
